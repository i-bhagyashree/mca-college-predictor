# Rank from percentage: Rank Predictor vs ML

Generated by `pipeline/ml_rank_from_percentage.py`. No cut-offs are used: the rank comes only from the merit lists.

**Data:** the official merit lists 2025 R1, 2026 R1, 2026 R2 (3,329 candidates, percentage and rank only).
Merit lists are not published for every year.

- **Rank Predictor (no ML):** the student's position in the **latest merit list of the same round**, × list size.
- **ML:** trained on **all three lists** (every candidate: percentage → position = candidates above ÷ list size),
  all lists counting equally. Seven models compared.

**Tests**
1. *Leave one list out:* hide one list, predict the rank for every percentage 50–95% (step 0.5) from the other two.
2. *Real use:* predict a list only from earlier lists (2026 R1 from 2025 R1; 2026 R2 from 2025 R1 + 2026 R1).

| Isotonic regression (monotonic) | 22.0 | 57.4 | 72.1% | 8.3 | 30.7 | 27.1 | 26.0 |

**Best ML model:** Isotonic regression (monotonic): 22.0 ranks (leave-one-out), 26.0 (real use).
**Rank Predictor:** 29.7 ranks (leave-one-out), 32.0 (real use).

**More data is not better:** adding the older list(s) to the ML training data gives 61.9 ranks instead of
22.0. The candidates of that year had a different spread of percentages.
