# Machine learning: rank from percentage

Generated by `pipeline/ml_rank_from_percentage.py`.

**Question:** a student gives their graduation percentage. Can a machine-learning model predict their merit rank
better than the Rank Predictor (the average of the student's position in the recent merit lists)?

**Data:** the official merit lists 2025 R1, 2026 R1, 2026 R2: 3,329 candidates, percentage and rank only.
Merit lists are not available for every year, so the model learns from these three lists.
**Target:** position = rank ÷ list size; predicted rank = position × list size.

**Test:** leave one list out. Train on two lists, predict the rank for every percentage from 50% to 95%
(step 0.5) in the third list, compare with the real rank. Done for each of the three lists.
This is the same test that gives the Rank Predictor its error.

| Model | Average error (ranks) | 90% of predictions within | Worst error | Within 25 ranks | Error on 2025 R1 | Error on 2026 R1 | Error on 2026 R2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rank Predictor (no ML: average position) | 21.7 | 57.0 | 93 | 73.2% | 7.4 | 30.9 | 26.7 |
| Linear regression | 35.6 | 99.0 | 151 | 52.9% | 30.8 | 34.8 | 41.2 |
| Polynomial regression (degree 3) | 35.6 | 71.4 | 160 | 51.5% | 21.1 | 44.0 | 41.7 |
| k-nearest neighbours (k = 25) | 22.8 | 59.7 | 96 | 72.1% | 9.5 | 30.4 | 28.6 |
| Decision tree | 22.8 | 59.0 | 99 | 69.9% | 10.3 | 29.6 | 28.4 |
| Random forest | 22.7 | 59.3 | 99 | 71.0% | 10.1 | 29.6 | 28.5 |
| Gradient boosting (monotonic) | 22.8 | 57.3 | 95 | 72.1% | 9.3 | 30.8 | 28.3 |
| Isotonic regression (monotonic) | 22.3 | 58.0 | 93 | 71.3% | 9.1 | 29.8 | 28.1 |

**Best ML model:** Isotonic regression (monotonic), average error 22.3 ranks.
**Rank Predictor:** 21.7 ranks.

**More data is not better here:** adding the older list(s) to the training data of the best model gives
61.5 ranks average error instead of 22.3. The candidates of 2020 had a different spread of
percentages, so old lists teach the model the wrong positions.
